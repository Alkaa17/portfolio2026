Our team evaluated Poe with three complementary methods: a comparative evaluation against five competitors, a heuristic audit based on Nielsen's principles, and usability testing with graduate students. The study surfaced key problems in onboarding, bot comparison, discoverability and error messaging.

I led the synthesis of our qualitative and quantitative findings into design improvements aimed at reducing drop-off, making features easier to find and streamlining bot interactions.

What makes Poe different

Poe combines many AI models under one roof, which creates some unusual UX challenges for first-time users on mobile.

  1. Explore trending prompts and bots

    Browse trending conversations and sample inputs.

    Reduces writer's block and inspires new tasks

  2. Create custom bots

    Build and publish personalized bots.

    Encourages creativity and repeated use

  3. Compare bot responses

    Send the same prompt to multiple bots.

    Helps users find the most useful output quickly
the problem

Powerful features, poor first-time experience

Poe offers powerful AI tools and customization, but first-time users struggle to understand what it does, how to choose a bot and how to start. As a result, its most distinctive features, comparison and bot creation, often go undiscovered.

  • 1
    No onboarding
    New users land in the app with no walkthrough or quick start.
  • 2
    Too many bots, unclear categories
    An unfiltered list with abstract labels makes choosing hard.
  • 3
    No tooltip or call to action for bot creation
    One of Poe's key differentiators stays hidden.

Phase 1

Comparative evaluation

We wanted to understand how Poe performs against similar and analogous tools on first-time experience, navigation and feature access.

We compared five competitors, ranging from direct rivals to analogous services, on the six dimensions listed here.

6 comparison dimensions

  1. AI models: organization and pricing
  2. UI: readability, layout and navigation
  3. UX: learnability and onboarding
  4. Customizability
  5. Response accuracy
  6. Cross-platform availability

Finding 1: no onboarding support for first-time users

Poe offers no onboarding or tooltips, unlike competitors such as ThinkBuddy and You.com. New users have to figure things out on their own, which makes the app harder to navigate from the start.

vs

Finding 2: key features are hard to discover

Tools like There's An AI For That use clear task categories. Poe relies on abstract terms like "Reasoning" and "Official," which makes it hard for users to know which bot to choose or where to start.

Poe's labels

Reasoning, Official

Describe the system, not the user's goal

Task-based labels

Write emails, Explain concepts

Start from what the user wants to get done

Finding 3: the homepage layout is overwhelming

Poe's homepage mixes bots, feeds and subscription prompts. Competitors keep the home screen focused. Poe's clutter leads to cognitive overload and hesitation to engage.

vs

What this means for Poe: powerful, but unapproachable

No guided entry points

Unlike competitors, Poe doesn't help users take a first step. There's no walkthrough or quick start.

Overloaded homepage

Mixing trending content, all bots and subscriptions creates confusion instead of clarity.

Missed personalization

Competitors tailor content by task, like "Write an email." Poe assumes users already know which bot to use and why.

Phase 2

Heuristic evaluation: 7 task areas, 10 heuristics

The researchers on our team judged Poe's compliance with recognized usability principles (Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, 1994). We identified the task categories to evaluate, broke each into smaller steps, then checked every step for heuristic violations and gave it a severity rating.

For example, the steps for Home Screen Navigation:

  1. Land on the homepage after opening the app
  2. Scan bot cards, trending prompts and UI sections
  3. Find links like "Create a Bot," "Help" or "Explore"
  4. Understand what each section represents
  5. Choose a logical next step

Severity scale

Select a rating to see what it means. Higher scores mean worse usability.

Where we looked

Our audit covered seven high-impact task areas. Each step was rated against Nielsen's 10 heuristics on the severity scale above. 60% of all evaluated tasks had minor or major usability issues, and the most friction-heavy areas were home navigation, bot creation and chatbot discovery.

Phase 3

What I took away

Combining three methods let each one check the others. The heuristic audit predicted where users would struggle, and testing showed exactly how badly, which made our recommendations much easier to defend.

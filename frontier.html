booking without the tricks

Frontier Airlines ticketing UX redesign

In collaboration with Sarosh Manzar, I redesigned Frontier Airlines' ticket booking flow to reduce manipulative patterns and build user trust. The existing system caused decision fatigue, hidden fees, and intrusive upsell pop-ups. We applied humane design principles to prioritize clarity, reduce friction, and empower informed decision-making.

Only price differences, not totals

Bundles are listed as "+$" differences, so users have to calculate the real price themselves before they can compare.

Prices in these recreations are illustrative.

These issues were addressed by centering the redesign around transparency, simplicity, and ethical interaction design.

analysis

Journey map and peak-end analysis

To prioritize improvements, we analyzed the current flow. Using journey mapping and peak-end analysis, we identified the high and low moments of the experience. Select a moment on the curve.

PeakInitial search via Google Flights shows promising low fares.

The user journey revealed a downward emotional trajectory beginning immediately after flight selection. This insight shaped the design priorities: persistent pricing visibility, fewer interruptions, and no hidden choices.

psych journey

Scoring the emotional cost of each step

To quantify emotional pain points, we mapped the psych journey step by step and scored each step on its psychological impact. Emotional highs were short-lived and quickly replaced by cognitive overload, dark patterns, and decision fatigue.

StepSelect a bar to see what drove the score.
  • The journey starts at +20 after seeing low fares, but falls to −50 due to confusing baggage flows and deceptive upsell pop-ups.
  • Key drop-off moments occurred during bundle selection, baggage selection, and the upsell stage.
  • Trust-breaking elements outweighed positive ones, especially in the middle and end of the flow.

This scoring framework gave us a structured way to identify which interface elements most harmed the experience.

persona

Designing for David

To guide our solution, we developed a user persona grounded in behavioral insights. Hover or tab through his goals and frustrations to see which feature answers each one.

Age38
OccupationOperations Manager
LocationChicago

David McGlone

"Is it too much to ask for a simple and honest booking experience?"

David is an organized Operations Manager based in Chicago. He values efficiency and a straightforward approach, including in travel. He flies a few times a year, mostly to visit family and friends and occasionally for short business trips. He's budget-minded and looks for good deals, but dislikes feeling nickel-and-dimed or tricked by hidden fees and manipulative tactics. He prefers clear information and a smooth, predictable experience that doesn't waste his time.

Goals
  • Clear total costEasily see the complete price of flights and bundles upfront.Answered by key feature 1: transparent pricing
  • Informed choicesUnderstand options without losing key comparison points.Answered by key feature 2: side-by-side comparison
  • Efficient bookingQuickly navigate without unnecessary steps or distractions.Answered by key feature 3: no pop-ups
Frustrations
  • Hidden chargesUnexpected fees added late in the booking process.Answered by key feature 1: transparent pricing
  • Confusing pricingHard to understand the actual cost of bundles and options.Answered by key feature 2: side-by-side comparison
  • Pushy upsellsConstant and irrelevant prompts to purchase upgrades.Answered by key feature 3: no pop-ups

With this understanding, we moved into designing a solution that directly addressed David's key frustrations.

solution

Three features, each answering a frustration

Key feature 1

Transparent pricing with baggage information

The original design misled users with "+[difference]" pricing. The redesign shows the total price for each fare (Basic, Economy, Business) upfront and prominently, so no one has to add base fares and extra charges in their head.

Baggage allowance now sits directly in the main search results instead of sub-menus, giving a complete and honest overview of the true cost and inclusions of each fare.

Key feature 2

Simplified side-by-side comparison

In the original, bundle information was scattered and needed scrolling, scanning, and mental effort. The redesign places features and total price for each bundle in a direct, adjacent layout, so users see the differences without losing context.

Seeing benefits and costs together gives users confidence and control over their selection, instead of feeling overwhelmed or misled.

Key feature 3

No pop-ups: a summary pop-up instead

Instead of frequent, disruptive upsell pop-ups, the redesign offers a subtle upgrade inside a contextual flight summary. It appears at a natural point, when users review their chosen flight before payment, rather than at every step.

Because users are already reviewing their booking, the upgrade feels like a helpful option rather than a sales tactic.

testing & iteration

A/B testing with six travelers

Goals

  • Test credibility of the new design. Does it improve perceived trustworthiness compared to the live site?
  • Understand perception of Frontier. How do users judge the brand's reliability through the current site versus the new design?
  • Enhance user experience. Find areas to improve by gauging satisfaction with the new design.

Methods

Usability testing with six participants from North America, ages 29 to 70, booking a Frontier flight on desktop. Tasks assessed how users interacted with pricing and bundle benefits, and which upsell pop-up felt clearer and more trustworthy.

Learnings

  • WorkedOverall, users found the booking process straightforward and appreciated the clutter-free layout.
  • GapSome struggled with the lack of filters for baggage-inclusive fares and a visually unengaging design.
  • MixedThe live site looked visually better but suffered from intrusive ads and upsells.
  • WorkedUsers preferred the redesigned upsell pop-up for its clearer details on premium benefits, though a few liked the original's appealing seat images.
  • SurpriseTrust was not a concern for everyone; some assumed Frontier was reputable by default.
  • GapBaggage filters, upfront bundle dropdowns, and a stronger upsell design could significantly improve the experience.

final design

Simplicity, transparency, and user autonomy

  • All-in total pricing, clear from the start
  • Bundle comparison stays visible throughout selection
  • Upsells integrated subtly within summary screens, not pop-ups
  • Color-coded bundle cards with hover states for clarity and scannability

The overall booking flow was reduced in complexity while preserving essential steps, supporting informed decisions without forced choices or artificial urgency.

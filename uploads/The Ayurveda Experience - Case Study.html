The Ayurveda Experience · Product & Growth Design

Designing for growth across the customer journey

At The Ayurveda Experience, I designed and optimized experiences across acquisition, conversion, and retention—connecting marketing, ecommerce, and product touchpoints into a more effective customer journey.

Over 20 months, I worked across web, mobile, email, push notifications, landing pages, checkout, post-purchase experiences, and experimentation—using behavioral data, A/B testing, customer insights, and continuous iteration to improve engagement and commercial performance.

Growth wasn't simply about getting people to click “Buy.”

The Ayurveda Experience sells Ayurvedic wellness and skincare products. Buying was rarely impulsive—it was research-heavy.

Before converting, customers looked for education, credibility, ingredient and benefit details, reviews, and reassurance. Taking those away to "speed things up" risked taking away the reasons to buy.

The brief was not “Make checkout shorter.”

The real design question

How might we increase conversion and retention without removing the education and trust customers needed to make confident purchase decisions?

01 — Acquire

Getting the right customers into the journey

The goal was never just more clicks. Each campaign had to balance curiosity, education, credibility, urgency, and relevance—because the people clicking still had a lot of research ahead of them.

Acquisition work spanned weekly email marketing, daily push notifications, promotional campaigns, Amazon A+ content, testimonial-led stories, and the landing-page entry points they all led to.

What we experimented with

  • Subject lines
  • Messaging hierarchy
  • Imagery
  • CTA language
  • CTA size
  • Content density
  • Promotional offers
  • Testimonial placement
  • Visual hierarchy
  • Product positioning
the takeaway

Visual design was not decorative—it was part of the conversion strategy.

02 — Educate

Helping customers feel informed enough to buy

Standard ecommerce advice

Cut the content. Shorten the path to purchase.

What research and behavior showed here

For this audience, education built confidence.

design principle
Optimize for informed decision-making, not simply fewer clicks.

04 — Retain

Designing beyond the first purchase

Acquiring a customer was only the first step. Long-term growth depended on repeat purchases and a relationship that continued after the order confirmation.

  • Order tracking
  • Post-purchase engagement
  • App adoption
  • Cross-sell
  • Replenishment
  • Notifications
  • Re-engagement
  • Retention campaigns

Post-purchase upsells

Complementary products recommended right after purchase, while intent was highest.

Replenishment campaigns

Emails timed to expected usage cycles, so reminders arrived before a product ran out.

A tracking page has one primary job

“Where is my order?”

Customers returned to it often, making it one of the highest-intent post-purchase touchpoints. The existing page showed shipment status and little else. The goal was to protect that core task while supporting confidence, support, discovery, app adoption, and repeat engagement.

New page order, by priority

  1. Order visibilityShipment status, immediately.
  2. Product discoveryBestsellers and relevant products.
  3. Trust buildingCustomer testimonials.
  4. Support accessCustomer care, without hunting.
  5. Retention channelsAn invitation to the app.

Business opportunities were introduced only after the customer's immediate need was satisfied.

Mobile app

Expanding the strategy into mobile

The app carried the same lifecycle thinking into a channel built for repeat visits: a redesigned navigation, stronger collection pages, promotional modules, and notifications that brought people back with app-exclusive offers.

Accessibility was part of optimization—not an afterthought

Many customers were older users reading long pages on every kind of device. Making content clearer and easier to read served accessibility and business performance at the same time.

  • TypographySizes and weights that hold up across long reads.
  • ReadabilityContent density and sectioning that make long pages scannable.
  • ContrastText and controls that stay legible over imagery.
  • SpacingRoom to breathe between dense blocks of information.
  • Touch targetsControls sized for real hands on real phones.
  • Content structureClear hierarchy so the key point is found first.
  • Responsive behaviorLayouts that keep their meaning on small screens.
  • CTA clarityButtons that say exactly what happens next.
  • Cognitive loadOne decision at a time, especially at checkout.

the bigger lesson

The biggest lesson wasn't that a single redesign created these outcomes. Growth came from continuously finding friction, testing interventions, and improving interconnected parts of the customer journey.

What this experience changed about how I design

01

Shorter isn't always better

Sometimes removing information removes the confidence needed to convert.

02

Design and business metrics are connected

Every interaction can influence engagement, trust, conversion, or retention.

03

Optimization is cumulative

Strong product experiences emerge from repeated observation, experimentation, measurement, and iteration.
